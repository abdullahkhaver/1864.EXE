Real problems, real deadlines, one Saturday.

1864.EXE brings high-school students from different schools and colleges to GC for a day of building. Teams pick one of four competitions, get their challenge in the morning and show what they made to the judges in the afternoon.

It's a chance to practise the things school rarely tests: solving an open problem with a team, using AI tools responsibly, and explaining your work to people who will ask hard questions.

Pick one of four competitions

Each student can enter only one, and each competition takes at most six teams.

Web development

Build a working website or web app for a challenge revealed on the day.

You'll hand in

  • A working demo, local or live
  • Your source files or repository
  • A 2–3 minute explanation

You can use

  • HTML, CSS and JavaScript
  • Frameworks and libraries
  • Public APIs and documentation

Prompt engineering

Get the best possible results from an AI model with clear, structured prompts.

Tasks may include

  • Pulling structured data out of text
  • Summarising and classifying
  • Rewriting content to a format
  • Creative and multi-step instructions

You'll hand in

  • Your final prompts and outputs
  • A short rationale
  • Earlier versions, when asked

Every team uses the same organiser-approved AI models.

UI/UX design

Turn a real user problem into an app or website design people find easy to use.

Expected work

  • User flows and wireframes
  • High-fidelity screens
  • A clickable prototype, if you can

You'll hand in

  • Your Figma (or similar) file
  • A 3-minute walkthrough

Tech pitch

Pitch your own tech product, startup or service to a panel of judges.

Cover these points

  • The problem and who has it
  • Your solution and its market
  • How it makes money or sustains itself
  • Whether it can be built, and first steps

On stage

  • 5-minute pitch
  • 3 minutes of judges' questions
  • A prototype helps, but isn't required

You can prepare your pitch before the day. It must be your own idea.

Challenge themes

Briefs for web, prompt and design teams are revealed on the morning of the event. They'll come from one of these areas:

  • Education and learning
  • Accessibility and inclusion
  • Campus and community life
  • Health, safety and well-being
  • Sustainability and the environment
  • Productivity, public service and social impact

How the day runs

Times are approximate and may shift a little on the day.

  1. Check-in

    Fee check, seating and laptop setup.

  2. Opening briefing

    Rules and how judging works.

  3. Challenges released

    Each competition gets its brief.

  4. Build time

    Web, prompt and design teams work on their briefs. Pitch teams check in and get ready.

  5. Submissions close

    Short break while judging is set up.

  7. Judging

    Results, certificates and prizes.

Rules

Short version: be a high-school student, do your own work, and be decent to everyone.

Who can enter

  • High-school students only. Grades 9–12, Matric, Intermediate, O and A Level, HSSC or equivalent. University students can't take part.
  • Teams of 2 to 4. Each student joins one team and one competition.
  • Register before the deadline with accurate details. Your place is confirmed after we check your eligibility and fee.
  • Bring your school or college ID, or other proof that you're currently enrolled.

Your work

  • Web, prompt and design work starts after the brief is released. Pitch teams may prepare beforehand.
  • Internet, documentation, open-source libraries and public APIs are allowed unless your competition's instructions say otherwise.
  • AI assistants are allowed for web and design teams if you say you used them. You must be able to explain everything you submit. Prompt teams use only the organisers' models.
  • Copied projects, plagiarism or claiming others' work can get your team disqualified.
  • Teachers and mentors can't do the work for you during competition hours.

On the day

  • Bring your own laptops and chargers.
  • No harassment, cheating, sabotage, impersonation or taking other teams' files.
  • No hacking of any kind. Scanning, testing or attacking GC systems, public services, other teams or anyone's device is strictly forbidden.
  • Follow GC rules and instructions from venue and security staff.

Judging and results

  • Submit before the deadline. Late work may lose marks or be refused, unless the organisers confirm a technical problem.
  • Judges may ask for a live demo, your source or design files, prompt history, the tools you used and why you made your choices.
  • Judges' scores and the committee's decisions are final after internal checks.
  • Your work stays yours. GC and the IT Society may show project titles, screenshots and event photos for publicity.

How you'll be scored

Each competition has its own marking scheme. Choose one to see it.

  • Does it work, and how well is it built?30%
  • Usefulness for the problem20%

  • Technical quality and your understanding of it15%
  • Originality and thoughtful use of tools10%
  • Demo and explanation10%

Prompt engineering

  • Task success and quality of output35%
  • Clear, well-structured prompts20%
  • Works across different cases and limits15%
  • Efficient, controlled iteration10%
  • Creativity and strategy10%
  • Explanation and responsible use10%

UI/UX design

  • User flow and usability25%
  • Understanding of the user and problem20%
  • Visual hierarchy and consistency20%
  • Accessibility and inclusiveness15%
  • How complete the prototype is10%
  • Design reasoning and presentation10%

Tech pitch

  • Business model and sustainability20%
  • The problem and why it matters15%
  • Innovation and value15%
  • Understanding of the market and alternatives15%
  • Technical feasibility15%
  • Pitch and communication10%
  • Answers to the judges' questions10%

Questions

Who can take part?

Students currently enrolled in Grades 9–12, Matric, Intermediate, O or A Level, or an equivalent qualification. University students can't enter.

How big can a team be?

Two to four students.

How much does it cost?

PKR 1,200 per team, not per person.

Can we enter more than one competition?

No. Each student takes part in one competition only.

Do we need our own laptops?

Yes. Bring your laptops, chargers and anything else you'll need.

Can we use AI tools?

Web and design teams can, as long as they say so and can explain their work. Prompt engineering teams all use the same organiser-approved models.

Can we prepare before the day?

Pitch teams can prepare their idea and slides beforehand. Web, prompt and design work has to start after the brief is released on the day.

Does sending the form confirm our place?

No. Your place is confirmed after we check your payment and eligibility.

How will we hear from you?

Through the contact details you give when registering, and the IT Society's official channels.
